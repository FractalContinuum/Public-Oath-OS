# Entry Oath Scheme

**Entry Oath Scheme — v0.1-provisional**

> This document presents a provisional institutional design proposal for applying the common core of Public Oath OS to entry screening, visa examination, and immigration-status administration.
> It does not constitute a demand directed at any particular state, a definitive interpretation of current law, or a completed institutional framework capable of immediate implementation.
> Any actual reference to or implementation of this proposal requires reconsideration and redesign in accordance with the constitution, laws, immigration system, judicial system, authentication framework, human-rights protections, and social environment of the relevant country or jurisdiction.

---

## 0. Position of This Proposal

This document is a derivative supplement to the `Public-Oath-OS-Institutional-Upgrade-Proposal`.

The main proposal applies oath protocols broadly to public office, authority, access to information, naturalization, and other national institutional fields.

This document applies the same common core to the more limited institutional fields of **entry screening and immigration-status administration**.

The two are connected through the following basic structure:

```text
Oath
↓
Assurance of identity, intent, signature, and authenticity
↓
Oath record
↓
Reasonable grounds for doubt
↓
Opportunity for explanation and rebuttal
↓
Verification of consistency with the oath
↓
Formal determination under applicable law
```

This document is not intended to criticize or evaluate any particular state.

It presents an institutional alternative for jurisdictions facing concerns regarding national security, false declarations, unlawful conduct, information verification, examination burdens, or related issues in entry screening.

The proposed alternative differs from broad ideological screening or unlimited information searches by using a limited and clearly defined oath made by the applicant as the starting point for examination and subsequent verification.

---

## 1. Problem Statement

States responsible for entry screening may face issues such as:

* false declarations concerning the purpose of entry;
* fraudulent acquisition of a visa or immigration status;
* serious unlawful conduct during a stay;
* violence, destruction, terrorism, espionage, or organized crime;
* unlawful provision of funds, goods, facilities, or information;
* intentionally false explanations in response to lawful inquiries;
* destruction of evidence or obstruction of an investigation;
* examination burdens arising from the review of large quantities of information;
* the risk of excessive intrusion into an applicant’s beliefs, opinions, or cultural background;
* the risk of arbitrary, discriminatory, or politically motivated selection.

In response to these concerns, some jurisdictions may consider reviewing publicly available information, online activity, personal relationships, travel history, or other broad categories of information.

However, an approach that continuously expands the range of information examined may produce the following problems:

* increased examination costs;
* unclear decision-making standards;
* an increasingly unclear boundary between conduct review and ideological screening;
* excessive interference with privacy and freedom of expression;
* arbitrary judgments by individual officers;
* political or diplomatic friction between states; and
* procedures in which the person concerned has no meaningful opportunity to rebut the information used.

This proposal responds to these problems not merely by expanding information collection, but by using a limited and explicit oath made by the applicant as the starting point for entry screening and subsequent verification.

---

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this proposal is not to expand surveillance powers without limitation.

It is to use the applicant’s own express oath as the basis for organizing later administrative or judicial responses, where reasonable grounds for doubt arise, in the following form:

> Verify the consistency between the oath made by the individual and specifically observed conduct.

This structure clarifies the purpose, subject matter, scope, and procedure of state intervention.

It may thereby reduce the explanatory and justificatory costs associated with necessary intervention.

At the same time, the proposal does not treat the mere existence of doubt as an automatic determination of violation or responsibility.

It incorporates an opportunity for explanation and rebuttal.

---

## 3. Basic Principles

### 3.1 Conduct, Not Belief

This proposal does not target thought, belief, religion, culture, ethnic identity, political opinion, or lifestyle as such.

It targets conduct that has been defined in advance in a limited and clear manner.

Examples include:

* intentional falsehood concerning the purpose of entry;
* material falsehood in a visa application or entry-screening procedure;
* specifically defined serious unlawful conduct during a stay;
* violence, destruction, terrorism, espionage, or organized crime;
* unlawful provision of funds, goods, facilities, or information;
* intentional false explanations in response to lawful inquiries; and
* destruction of evidence or obstruction of a lawful investigation.

Where comprehensive phrases such as “do not harm the national interest” or “respect the culture” are used, their meaning must be divided into concrete categories of conduct.

---

### 3.2 Minimum Necessary Oath Content

The content of the oath must be limited to matters reasonably related to entry, stay, and the purpose of the relevant immigration status.

The oath must not be used for:

* compulsory conformity to a particular ideology;
* unconditional support for a government;
* forced cultural assimilation;
* suppression of legitimate political criticism;
* restriction of lawful religious activity or expression; or
* selection based on race, nationality, ethnicity, or other personal attributes.

---

### 3.3 Distinguishing Refusal from Character Judgment

Where an oath is lawfully established as an entry requirement, refusal may be treated as a failure to satisfy that procedural requirement.

However, refusal alone must not be used to label a person as dangerous, criminal, hostile, dishonest, or disloyal.

The subject of the determination is not the person’s character.

It is whether the applicable institutional requirement has been satisfied.

---

### 3.4 Distinguishing Strong Presumption from Final Liability

A freely made oath, together with a record that adequately assures the identity of the oath-taker and the authenticity of the act, may later possess substantial evidentiary force concerning knowledge, intent, or accountability where conduct appears inconsistent with the oath.

This document refers to such a factual relationship as a **Strong-Presumption Fact**.

However, a Strong-Presumption Fact does not automatically establish:

* criminal liability;
* administrative liability;
* removal or deportation;
* revocation of immigration status; or
* any other legal consequence.

Final determination must be made under the applicable law of the relevant jurisdiction and in accordance with the required evidence, authority, and due process.

---

## 4. Entry Oath Unit

### 4.1 Minimum Core of the Oath

The minimum Entry Oath Unit may consist of the following three elements:

1. There is no intentional falsehood concerning the purpose of entry, the visa application, or other material declarations.
2. During the stay, the oath-taker will not engage in serious unlawful conduct that has been defined clearly in advance.
3. Where reasonable grounds for doubt arise, the oath-taker will participate, within the lawful scope of the procedure, in explanation and consistency verification.

Additional clauses may be adopted according to:

* immigration status;
* purpose of stay;
* access privileges; or
* other relevant conditions.

Any additional clause must remain reasonably related to the purpose of entry or stay.

---

### 4.2 Example Oath

The oath should be as concise and clear as possible.

Example:

> I declare that I have not intentionally made any false statement concerning the purpose of my entry or any material matter declared in this procedure.
> I undertake to comply with the applicable law during my stay and not to engage in serious unlawful conduct identified in advance.
> If reasonable grounds arise to question the consistency between my declaration or oath and specifically observed facts, I will participate in explanation and verification conducted under applicable law and due process.

The actual wording should be adjusted according to:

* the relevant jurisdiction;
* immigration status;
* age;
* language ability; and
* the nature of the procedure.

---

### 4.3 Comprehensibility

The system must ensure that the oath-taker can understand the content and legal or administrative meaning of the oath.

Possible measures include:

* multilingual versions;
* plain-language summaries;
* separation of the formal oath from the explanatory text;
* interpretation;
* oral reading;
* opportunities to ask questions; and
* special support for minors or persons requiring assistance.

An oath obtained through a merely formal signature where the person could not understand its content loses credibility as a Strong-Presumption Fact.

---

## 5. Procedural Flow

### 5.1 Advance Notice

The existence and general content of the oath system should be disclosed in advance at an appropriate stage, such as:

* visa application;
* travel authorization;
* airline ticket reservation;
* electronic travel authorization; or
* another pre-entry procedure.

Advance notice should include:

* the conditions under which the oath is required;
* the content of the oath;
* the procedural effect of refusal;
* the information that will be recorded;
* the retention period;
* the purpose of use; and
* the available explanation, review, and appeal procedures.

---

### 5.2 Review of the Oath

The applicant reviews the oath before or during entry screening.

The oath may be presented through:

* paper documents;
* electronic terminals;
* online systems; or
* another suitable method.

Where necessary, the applicant must be given an opportunity to request:

* clarification;
* translation;
* interpretation; or
* further explanation.

---

### 5.3 Assurance of Identity and Authenticity

The identity, free will, and authenticity of the applicant’s signature or equivalent act must be assured through a method appropriate to the relevant jurisdiction.

Possible methods include:

* face-to-face confirmation by an immigration officer or other authorized official;
* notarization or a function equivalent to notarization;
* a statutory oath procedure;
* electronic signature;
* a combination of biometric authentication and an electronic record;
* connection with identity verification performed during the visa process; and
* timestamping of the signed document.

This proposal does not prescribe a single authentication method.

The objective is to establish a verifiable record of:

* who made the oath;
* when it was made;
* what content was understood; and
* what declaration of intent was expressed.

---

### 5.4 Finalization of the Oath Record

After completion of the oath, a minimum necessary oath record is registered.

Potential record fields include:

* identity information;
* date and time of the oath;
* version of the oath document;
* applicable oath clauses;
* authentication method;
* hash of the oath document;
* period of validity;
* related visa or immigration status; and
* record-management authority.

The full oath document should not be shared without limitation.

Information sharing should be restricted to what is necessary to verify authenticity and the applicable clauses.

---

### 5.5 Entry Screening

The oath does not replace ordinary entry screening.

It operates together with statutory examination of:

* passport validity;
* visa status;
* purpose of stay;
* grounds for refusal of entry; and
* other applicable requirements.

Completion of the oath does not automatically guarantee admission.

Nor may the oath system be used to expand existing examination standards without limitation.

---

### 5.6 Post-Entry Connection

Where reasonable grounds arise after entry to question the consistency between the oath and specific facts, the oath record may be used as the starting point for consistency verification.

However, the oath record alone must not serve as a basis for initiating permanent surveillance where there are no reasonable grounds for doubt, no lawful authority, or no demonstrated necessity.

---

## 6. Refusal and Inability to Take the Oath

### 6.1 Refusal

Where the oath is established as a lawful entry requirement, refusal may be treated as failure to satisfy that requirement.

Possible procedural consequences may include:

* non-acceptance of an application;
* refusal of a visa;
* denial of entry; or
* another procedure authorized by law.

The system should clearly state:

* the specific consequence of refusal;
* whether reapplication is permitted;
* the available appeal or review procedure;
* remedies where explanation or translation was inadequate; and
* emergency, humanitarian, refugee, or other exceptions.

---

### 6.2 Capacity to Take the Oath

Reasonable accommodation must be provided for persons who cannot understand or express the oath through the ordinary procedure because of:

* age;
* disability;
* illness;
* language ability; or
* another relevant circumstance.

Inability to use the ordinary procedure must not automatically be treated as refusal.

Possible accommodations include:

* a legal representative;
* an assistant;
* an interpreter;
* simplified language;
* audio or visual explanation;
* partial exemption; or
* an alternative verification procedure.

---

### 6.3 Humanitarian Exceptions

Different procedures may be established for:

* refugee claims;
* emergency evacuation;
* serious threats to life or physical safety;
* family reunification; or
* other humanitarian circumstances.

The oath system must not be used as a formal mechanism for excluding humanitarian protection.

---

## 7. Oath-Breach Verification Framework

### 7.1 Basic Structure

Intervention concerning an entrant should not be structured as:

> Monitor the person because the person appears suspicious.

It should instead be structured as:

> Verify the consistency between the oath made by the individual and specifically observed facts.

However, the existence of the oath itself must not serve as a general and permanent basis for surveillance.

Verification must be limited to situations where:

* reasonable grounds for doubt exist;
* a lawful authority exists;
* the measure is necessary; and
* the measure is proportionate.

---

### 7.2 V0: Record State

This is the normal state in which the oath record remains validly preserved.

Unless specific grounds for doubt arise, use of the record is limited to preservation and minimum necessary verification.

No additional investigation or surveillance begins.

---

### 7.3 V1: Consistency Screening

At V1, the authority determines whether there is a reasonable connection between:

* specifically observed facts; and
* a particular oath clause.

The following matters should be identified:

* the specific conduct at issue;
* the potentially relevant oath clause;
* the source of the information;
* the reliability of the information;
* the possibility of mistaken identity;
* the relationship to the declared purpose of entry or stay; and
* the need for further verification.

Where no concrete connection to an oath clause can be shown, the procedure should, in principle, terminate at V1.

---

### 7.4 V2: Explanation and Limited Verification

Where V1 identifies a reasonable connection, the individual must be given an opportunity to explain and rebut the information.

Limited verification may be conducted where permitted by law.

Possible measures include:

* requests for explanation;
* interviews;
* requests for documents;
* reconfirmation of the oath;
* limited inquiries to relevant institutions; and
* limited investigation satisfying necessity and proportionality.

Where the explanation resolves the doubt, the procedure returns to V0.

The scope of verification must be limited to matters related to:

* the oath clause at issue; and
* the specific observed facts.

---

### 7.5 V3: Formal Determination and Enforcement

Where reasonable grounds for doubt remain unresolved after an opportunity for explanation and rebuttal, and the requirements of applicable law are satisfied, the matter may proceed to a formal determination by an authorized institution.

Relevant matters may include:

* whether the oath was breached;
* whether a falsehood was intentional;
* whether the person understood the oath;
* the relationship between the oath and the observed conduct;
* the lawfulness of information acquisition;
* the reliability of the evidence;
* statutory grounds for revocation of entry or immigration status;
* connection with criminal or administrative law; and
* the necessity and proportionality of the proposed measure.

Coercive measures must not be triggered automatically on the basis of suspicion alone.

Removal, revocation of immigration status, criminal proceedings, or other measures must be based on the statutory requirements and due process of the relevant jurisdiction.

---

## 8. Data and Information Governance

### 8.1 Purpose Limitation

Oath records must be used only for expressly stated purposes, including:

* entry and immigration procedures;
* identity verification;
* verification of oath content; and
* other clearly defined purposes.

Use for another purpose requires:

* a legal basis;
* necessity;
* notice to the individual; and
* an appropriate oversight structure.

---

### 8.2 Data Minimization

The information retained must be limited to the minimum necessary to achieve the institutional purpose.

The existence of the oath system must not be used to justify continuous collection of unlimited:

* personal information;
* social-media information;
* ideological information; or
* information concerning personal relationships.

---

### 8.3 Retention Period

The retention period should be determined in light of:

* the visa period;
* immigration status;
* length of stay;
* statutory record-keeping obligations; and
* the possibility of dispute.

After the retention period expires, the data should be:

* deleted;
* anonymized; or
* otherwise processed appropriately.

---

### 8.4 Access Control

The system must control:

* who may access the oath record;
* the scope of access authority;
* the purpose of access; and
* access history.

Clear responsibility must be established for:

* unauthorized access;
* unauthorized use; and
* unauthorized sharing.

---

### 8.5 Correction and Review

The individual must have access to a procedure for correcting errors in:

* the oath record;
* identity information; or
* related data.

The individual must also have access, as appropriate under the relevant jurisdiction, to:

* an objection procedure;
* administrative review; or
* judicial review

concerning determinations or measures relating to an alleged oath breach.

---

## 9. Safeguards

### 9.1 Prohibition on Ideological Screening

The oath system must not be used to evaluate:

* thought;
* belief;
* religion;
* political opinion;
* cultural background; or
* emotional attitudes toward a state.

The subject of evaluation must be limited to:

* clearly defined conduct; and
* declarations made in the entry or immigration procedure.

---

### 9.2 Prohibition on Unlimited Surveillance

Completion of the oath does not justify:

* continuous surveillance;
* comprehensive information collection; or
* indefinite behavioral tracking.

Verification must be limited to circumstances where reasonable grounds based on specific facts arise.

---

### 9.3 Prohibition on Discriminatory Operation

The content of the oath or intensity of verification must not be altered arbitrarily solely on the basis of:

* nationality;
* ethnicity;
* race;
* religion;
* language; or
* another personal attribute.

Different treatment requires:

* a clear legal basis;
* a concrete risk;
* necessity; and
* proportionality.

---

### 9.4 Separation of Doubt from Violation Determination

The following must not be treated as equivalent to a final determination of an oath breach:

* the existence of doubt;
* the making of an inquiry; or
* the commencement of limited verification.

The stages from V0 to V3 must remain clearly distinguished.

---

### 9.5 Prevention of Misuse of Strong-Presumption Facts

Any actor that publishes, reports, quotes, or evaluates:

* doubt concerning an oath breach; or
* a Strong-Presumption Fact

must accurately represent:

* the content;
* the scope;
* the evidence; and
* the procedural stage.

The following must not be conflated:

* reasonable grounds for doubt;
* ongoing verification;
* formal determination; and
* final legal liability.

This principle does not impose prior restraint on:

* reporting;
* criticism;
* expression; or
* legitimate whistleblowing.

It requires accountability where Strong-Presumption Facts are handled inaccurately.

---

## 10. Implementation Model

### 10.1 Small-Scale Pilot

At the initial stage, a voluntary or limited pilot may be conducted within:

* a specified immigration-status category;
* a particular airport or port;
* an electronic travel authorization system; or
* another limited procedural setting.

Evaluation criteria may include:

* understanding of the oath;
* procedural time;
* cost;
* accuracy of identity verification;
* authenticity of records;
* number of explanation requests;
* rate of mistaken identification;
* rate at which verification ends without enforcement;
* impact on human rights and privacy; and
* effectiveness of review and appeal procedures.

---

### 10.2 Phased Introduction

Based on the pilot results, the system should adjust:

* the target population;
* oath language;
* authentication method;
* data governance; and
* verification procedure.

A phased introduction may follow the structure below:

```text
Voluntary oath
↓
Pilot within a limited procedure
↓
Application to specified immigration statuses or access privileges
↓
Independent evaluation
↓
Institutional revision
↓
Full introduction only to the extent that necessity is demonstrated
```

---

### 10.3 Connection with Existing Systems

This proposal does not replace existing systems for:

* entry screening;
* visas;
* immigration status;
* electronic travel authorization;
* passport verification;
* identity verification; or
* administrative sanctions.

It adds the following connection points to existing systems:

* a clear oath;
* assurance of identity and intent;
* a verifiable oath record;
* defined triggers for reasonable doubt;
* an opportunity for explanation and rebuttal;
* limited consistency verification;
* a formal determination procedure; and
* responsibility for the handling of Strong-Presumption Facts.

---

## 11. Scope of the Proposal

This proposal is not limited to any single state.

It is made publicly available as a structure that may be reviewed by Japan or any other jurisdiction facing issues concerning:

* entry screening;
* visa examination;
* immigration-status administration;
* national security;
* false declarations; or
* examination costs.

However, the following differ across jurisdictions:

* legal effect of an oath;
* authority of administrative institutions;
* authentication methods;
* revocation of immigration status;
* removal or deportation;
* criminal liability; and
* data-protection law.

Accordingly, any implementation in another jurisdiction must separate:

* the abstract structure; from
* jurisdiction-specific institutional elements

and redesign the proposal in accordance with the applicable legal and institutional environment.

## 12. NO-Capture / NO-Animus

This proposal is not motivated by hostility toward any particular:

* state;
* people;
* ethnic group;
* culture;
* religion;
* political force; or
* immigrant community.

Nor is it intended to allow any particular ideology, political party, government, company, or authority to obtain exclusive Capture over the Entry Oath Scheme.

The proposal focuses not on the attributes of a person or group, but on:

* declarations concerning entry and stay;
* oaths;
* specific conduct;
* explanation; and
* verification procedures.

Institutional criticism and institutional proposals are made for the following purposes:

* clarifying examination standards;
* making the exercise of state authority explainable;
* guaranteeing an opportunity for rebuttal;
* restraining unlimited surveillance;
* connecting specific conduct with responsibility; and
* supporting institutional trust through verifiable procedures.
